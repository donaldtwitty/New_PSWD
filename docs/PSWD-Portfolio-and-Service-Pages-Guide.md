# Purposeful Solutions Web Design
## Portfolio Section & Service Pages — Copy + Structure Guide

This guide gives you ready-to-use copy and a clear build structure for two upgrades: a real **Portfolio / Work section** and four standalone **Service pages**. Copy is written in your existing brand voice (purpose-driven, mission-led, veteran values). Anywhere you see `[brackets]`, swap in real specifics — never publish placeholder numbers.

---

# PART 1 — THE PORTFOLIO SECTION

## 1.1 Where it lives

Add **Work** to your main nav, between *Process* and *Services*. Keep your current logo wall, but demote it: rename it "Trusted By" and make it a thin strip. The logo wall says *who* hired you; the portfolio proves *what you delivered*. Prospects buy on the second.

Use **real URLs, not modals.** Each project gets its own page:

- `/work` — the grid of all projects
- `/work/d-and-c-rental-portal`
- `/work/fam-logistics`
- `/work/philadelphia-sda-church`
- etc.

Real pages can rank in search, can be shared directly in a proposal or text, and let you link to them from service pages. The D&C content currently buried in a modal is exactly the kind of thing that should be a full indexed page.

## 1.3 Layout

**The project card** (used on homepage band and `/work` grid) shows:

- Project thumbnail (a clean desktop + mobile mockup, not a flat screenshot)
- Project name
- Client type — e.g. "Medical Courier Company" or "Faith-Based Organization"
- One-line outcome — the single most compelling result
- "View Case Study →"

**Example card:**

> Property Management · Full-Stack Web App

> View Case Study →

## 1.4 The case study page template

Use this same eight-part structure for every project. Consistency makes you look systematic — which is the whole pitch.

1. **Hero** — Project name, client type, and one headline result stat. Large mockup image.
2. **The Challenge** — 2–3 sentences. What problem did the client have *before* you?
3. **What We Did** — 3–5 short points describing your approach (strategy, design, build).
4. **The Solution** — Screenshots: at least one desktop and one mobile view. Caption each.
5. **The Results** — Concrete outcomes. Numbers if you have them; specific qualitative wins if you don't.
6. **Tech & Deliverables** — What you actually shipped (pages, features, stack, integrations).
7. **Client Voice** — A pull-quote testimonial from that client.
8. **CTA band** — "Want results like this? Let's talk."

## 1.8 A note on metrics

If you didn't track before/after numbers on past projects, you can still build credible Results sections:

- **Qualitative wins are valid** — "first-ever online giving," "replaced 3 tools with 1," "site visitors can now self-serve."
- **Ask the client** — a quick email ("have you noticed more inquiries since launch?") often surfaces a usable stat.
- **Going forward, baseline everything** — before launch, record current traffic, form submissions, and bounce rate. Six months later you'll have real numbers, and real numbers are what close deals.

# PART 2 — THE SERVICE PAGES

## 2.1 Why these need to be separate pages

Right now your four services are links that go nowhere (`#`). Each should be a full page at its own URL:

- `/services/custom-web-design`
- `/services/website-maintenance`
- `/services/seo-optimization`
- `/services/brand-identity`

Two reasons. First, **SEO**: a single page can't rank for "custom web design" *and* "website maintenance" *and* "SEO services" — but four focused pages each can. Second, **depth**: a prospect interested in maintenance wants to read about maintenance, not scroll past three other services to find it.

## 2.2 Shared service page template

Every service page follows the same eight sections:

1. **Hero** — Service name + an outcome-focused subhead + a CTA button.
2. **Who it's for** — 2–3 sentences naming the type of client and the situation they're in.
3. **What you get** — The concrete deliverables and inclusions.
4. **How it works** — A short version of your Discover → Design → Develop → Launch → Maintain process, tailored to this service.
5. **Why Purposeful Solutions** — Your differentiators (veteran-owned, purpose-driven strategy, collaborative process, software-engineering background).
6. **Related work** — Pull 1–2 relevant case studies from the portfolio.
7. **FAQ** — 3–5 real questions. Excellent for SEO and removes buying friction.
8. **CTA band** — "Ready to start? Let's talk."

# PART 3 — LOCAL SEO: TARGETING SAN ANTONIO

You're based in San Antonio, so your local SEO should target San Antonio — but there's one signal to address first.

**The phone number.** Your site uses a 915 number, which is the El Paso area code, while you operate out of San Antonio. To search engines and to local prospects, that's a mixed message. Two clean options: (1) get a 210 (San Antonio) number for the site while keeping 915 as a forwarding line, or (2) keep 915 but make your San Antonio location explicit everywhere so there's no ambiguity. Either is fine — just be consistent.

**The fundamentals to put in place:**

- **Google Business Profile** — claim and complete it. This is the single biggest local-search lever and it's free. Use your real business name, San Antonio location/service area, hours, and services.
- **NAP consistency** — your Name, Address (or service area), and Phone must be identical on your website, Google Business Profile, LinkedIn, and any directory. Inconsistency hurts local ranking.
- **Location signals on-site** — add "San Antonio, TX" to your footer, contact section, and ideally an "Areas We Serve" line. Consider one focused page like *Web Design in San Antonio* that genuinely speaks to local businesses.
- **LocalBusiness schema markup** — add structured data to your homepage so search engines clearly understand your name, location, and service area.
- **Reviews** — ask happy clients to leave a Google review. Reviews drive both ranking and trust.
